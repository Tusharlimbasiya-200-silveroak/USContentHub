Best Cheap Eats in Every Major US City: $10 or Less Meals

📅 2026-05-25 ⏱️ 2 min read 📝 543 words

Traveling doesn't mean eating $40 hotel breakfasts and $25 airport sandwiches. Every major US city has incredible food for $10 or less — you just need to know where locals actually eat. These picks are the real deal: delicious, filling, and wallet-friendly.

Best Cheap Eats in New York City Under $10

  • $1 Pizza Slice — NYC dollar slice shops serve hot, fresh pizza 24/7. Two slices and a drink = $3-4. It's not gourmet, but it's quintessential New York.
  • Halal Cart ($7-8) — Chicken over rice from street carts like The Halal Guys. Massive portions, incredible flavor.
  • Xi'an Famous Foods ($8-10) — Hand-pulled noodles with spicy lamb. Multiple locations. Worth every penny.

Best Budget Meals in Los Angeles

  • Taco Trucks ($2-3 per taco) — LA has the best tacos in America. Three carne asada tacos for $7-9 will change your life.
  • In-N-Out Burger ($5-7) — The classic. Double-Double, fries, and a drink for under $8.
  • Grand Central Market ($6-10) — Food hall downtown with incredible variety. Egg sandwiches, Thai food, tacos, all under $10.

Where to Eat Cheap in Chicago Like a Local

  • Italian Beef Sandwich ($8-10) — Al's #1 Italian Beef or Portillo's. Get it "dipped" with giardiniera peppers.
  • Deep Dish Pizza Slice ($5-6) — Not a whole pie — just a slice. Lou Malnati's and Giordano's sell by the slice at some locations.
  • Maxwell Street Polish ($5) — A Maxwell Street Polish sausage with grilled onions and mustard. A Chicago street food legend.
💡 Pro Tip: Google "[city name] cheap eats" + check Reddit's r/FoodPorn or r/[city name] subreddit. Locals share hidden gems that never show up on Yelp's first page.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

The ideal time depends on your destination and preferences. Generally, shoulder seasons (spring and fall) offer the best combination of good weather, smaller crowds, and lower prices. Avoid major holiday weekends for better deals.

A comfortable daily budget in the US ranges from $100-200 per person for mid-range travel. This includes accommodation ($80-150/night), food ($30-50/day), and activities ($20-50/day). Budget travelers can manage on $50-80/day.

Pack layers regardless of season, comfortable walking shoes, a reusable water bottle, phone charger, and any necessary medications. Check weather forecasts a week before your trip. Roll clothes to save space and always pack a rain jacket.

For trips under 300 miles, driving is usually cheaper, especially for groups. For longer distances, compare flight prices (book 3-6 weeks ahead for best deals) vs. gas costs. Factor in rental car costs and parking fees at your destination.

For popular destinations and national parks, reservations are highly recommended, especially during summer and holidays. Book accommodations 2-3 months ahead and restaurant reservations 2-4 weeks ahead for popular spots.
