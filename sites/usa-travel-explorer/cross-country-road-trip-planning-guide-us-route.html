How to Plan a Cross-Country Road Trip Across America

📅 2026-05-26 ⏱️ 2 min read 📝 507 words

A cross-country road trip is the ultimate American experience. Coast to coast, you'll drive through deserts, mountains, prairies, forests, and small towns that showcase America's incredible diversity. Here's how to plan one that's epic, not exhausting.

Choosing Your Route

Northern Route (I-90/I-94): NYC → Chicago → Badlands → Yellowstone → Seattle. Best June-September. Highlights: Chicago deep dish, Mount Rushmore, Glacier National Park, Pacific Northwest.

Southern Route (I-40/I-10): DC → Nashville → Memphis → Route 66 → Grand Canyon → LA. Best March-May or October-November. Highlights: BBQ, live music, desert landscapes, national parks.

Central Route (I-70/I-80): NYC → Pittsburgh → Indianapolis → Denver → Salt Lake City → San Francisco. Highlights: Rocky Mountains, Utah parks, Golden Gate Bridge.

Allow 10-14 days minimum. You CAN drive coast-to-coast in 4 days, but you'll hate every minute. The point is the journey, not the speed.

Budget

Average costs for two people, 14 days:

  • Gas: ~3,000 miles × $0.15/mile (30 mpg at $4.50/gal) = $450
  • Lodging: Mix of budget hotels ($80/night × 10 nights) + camping ($30/night × 4 nights) = $920
  • Food: $50-75/day for two = $700-1,050
  • Activities: Park passes, attractions, experiences = $200-400
  • Total: $2,300-2,800 for two people, 14 days. That's $80-100/person/day — cheaper than most vacations.

Vehicle Prep

  • Oil change, tire rotation, and fluid check before departure
  • Check tire pressure and tread depth (including spare)
  • Windshield wipers — you'll drive through rain at some point
  • AAA membership ($60-120/year) for roadside assistance across all 50 states
  • A phone mount — essential for navigation. Never hold your phone while driving.

Packing Essentials

  • Cooler with drinks and snacks (saves $10-20/day vs. gas station stops)
  • Paper map or downloaded offline maps (cell service is spotty in rural areas)
  • Layers of clothing — you'll pass through multiple climate zones
  • First-aid kit, flashlight, and jumper cables
  • Reusable water bottles (fill at gas stations and hotels for free)
  • Sunglasses and sunscreen — windshield driving means hours in the sun

Daily Driving Tips

  • Drive 3-5 hours max per day. More than that becomes miserable. Plan 200-300 miles/day with stops.
  • Start driving by 8 AM. Early driving means you arrive at destinations by early afternoon with time to explore.
  • Stop at every interesting sign. "World's Largest Ball of Twine" is cheesy but those roadside stops become the memories you talk about forever.
  • Gas up at half tank. In the Western US, gas stations can be 50-100 miles apart.
  • Switch drivers every 2 hours if you have multiple drivers.

Must-Stop Experiences

  • A diner in every state — local diners serve the best regional food
  • At least one national park (get the $80 annual pass)
  • A small town you've never heard of — just pull off the highway and explore
  • A state or county fair if your timing aligns
  • A sunset from a scenic overlook
🎯 Key Takeaway: Plan 10-14 days, drive 200-300 miles/day max, and budget $80-100/person/day. Choose your route based on season and interests (Northern for summer/nature, Southern for music/desert/parks). The best parts of a cross-country trip aren't the destinations — they're the unplanned stops at roadside diners, small towns, and "World's Largest Whatever" signs. Get your car checked before leaving, pack a cooler, and embrace the detours.
